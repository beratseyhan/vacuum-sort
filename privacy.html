Vacuum Sort – Privacy Policy

A fast, colourful ball-catching arcade game.

Who we are

Vacuum Sort is made by Berat Seyhan, an independent developer. Contact: beratseyhan@icloud.com.

In short

No account and no sign-in. Your progress stays on your device and, on iPhone and iPad, in your own iCloud, so it can follow you to your other devices. Ads are provided by Unity Ads through Unity LevelPlay. On iPhone and iPad, purchases are handled by Apple and managed through RevenueCat, and installs, play milestones, ad revenue and purchases are measured by Tenjin so we can tell which of our own ads brought players to the game. We run no servers, use no crash-reporting service of our own and do not sell personal data. The Android version differs in a few points: see “Android app (Google Play)” below.

Data stored on your device

The game saves your level progress, stars and scores, coins and boosters, settings (language, sound), which tips you have seen, and whether you own “No Ads”. It stays on your device and is never sent to us. If you use iCloud, the game also keeps a copy of your progress, coins and boosters in your own iCloud account (Apple’s iCloud Key-Value Store), so they carry over to your other devices and a reinstall; we cannot read it. It can also be part of your device’s own backup (for example iCloud Backup). Deleting the app deletes the data from the device; you can turn off iCloud for the game in your device’s settings.

Advertising (Unity Ads and Unity LevelPlay)

The free game shows a banner on the level map, occasional ads between levels (never during play), and reward videos that you can choose to watch for a free booster, a continue or double coins. On both iPhone/iPad and Android the ads are served through Unity LevelPlay by Unity Ads (Unity Technologies and its subsidiary ironSource Mobile; LevelPlay can also fill an ad from ironSource’s own exchange). Google AdMob is no longer used (since version 1.1.0). LevelPlay and Unity Ads may collect and process: your device’s advertising identifier (on iOS only if you allow tracking; on Android you can reset or delete it in Android’s settings), the identifier for vendors on iOS, IP address and the approximate location derived from it, device and operating-system information (such as model, OS version, language, screen size and network type), app information, the ads shown and your interactions with them, and diagnostic data of the ad SDK. They use it to show, measure and personalise ads and to prevent fraud.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (and when the game cannot tell your country), the game asks you before any ad is requested whether ads may be personalised; both answers are offered equally and the ads start only after you answer. Elsewhere ads are personalised unless you switch this off. You can change your choice at any time under “Privacy options” in the game’s settings; saying no also sends a “do not sell or share” signal. On iPhone and iPad, Apple’s App Tracking Transparency prompt then asks whether Vacuum Sort may track you; if you decline, the advertising identifier is not shared and the game works the same.

More: Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy · ironSource privacy policy.

In-app purchases

Purchases (No Ads, coin and booster packs) are processed by Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy. We never receive your payment details. Purchases go through RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.), a purchase-management service that checks them with Apple, so that “No Ads” can come back after a reinstall or on another device and so we can see sales figures. RevenueCat receives a random, anonymous app user ID created by the game (kept in your iCloud so your devices share it), the App Store transaction details of your purchases, and basic technical information (such as app version, operating-system version, device model, language and IP address). It does not receive your name, email or payment details. See the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

Measuring our own ads (Tenjin)

To learn which of our advertising campaigns (for example on Apple Search Ads, Facebook, Instagram or TikTok) brought a player to Vacuum Sort, and whether those campaigns pay for themselves, the game uses Tenjin (Tenjin, Inc.), a mobile attribution service. Tenjin receives: the time of the install and of each app launch, a random installation identifier, your device’s advertising identifier only if you allow tracking in Apple’s prompt, the identifier for vendors, IP address and the approximate country derived from it, device model, operating-system version, app version and language, play milestones (levels started, completed or failed, the first level finished, rewarded videos watched, days you came back), the estimated revenue of each ad shown to you, and the App Store transaction details of purchases (product, price, currency, transaction identifier and the App Store receipt, which contains no payment details). Tenjin matches installs with the ad networks’ click and view data, with Apple Search Ads attribution and with Apple’s SKAdNetwork (which reports attributions without identifying you), and it may share aggregated or event-level results with the ad network that ran the campaign. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Tenjin only measures you if you said yes to personalised ads in the game’s privacy question; you can change that choice under “Privacy options”. See the Tenjin Privacy Policy.

Android app (Google Play)

The Android version of Vacuum Sort uses the same ads and the same privacy question as the iPhone and iPad app (see “Advertising” above), but differs in three ways. No tracking SDK: Tenjin is not part of the Android app. No purchases: the Android app currently sells nothing (no in-app purchases, no RevenueCat). Saving: there is no iCloud on Android; your progress stays on the device and may be included in your device’s own Google backup.

Game engine

Vacuum Sort is built with the Unity engine. Unity Technologies may receive anonymous technical information about the device the game runs on (such as device model, operating system, processor, graphics hardware and screen size) to improve the engine. It contains no game progress and nothing you enter. See the Unity Privacy Policy.

Children

Vacuum Sort is a game for a general audience and is not designed for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your choices and rights

We hold no personal data about you on any server, so there is nothing for us to export or delete. For data processed by Unity (LevelPlay, Unity Ads), RevenueCat or Tenjin, use “Privacy options” in the game, your device’s tracking and advertising settings, or contact those companies through the policies linked above. You can contact us at any time with questions.

Changes

If the game starts handling data differently, this page will be updated and the date below will change.
